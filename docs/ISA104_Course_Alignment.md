# ISA104 w1-w9 prior-teaching alignment (audit v1.1)

Eight PDF decks, 335 pages, four lecturers (Dapeng Dong, Haoran Wen,
Kai Pfeiffer, Yu Liu) were compared against Lecture 10, Lab 5 and the
two-hour English session. Full evidence with page numbers is in the
instructor-only audit report; this file is the public summary.

## Verdict

**No topic conflict.** The official Week 1 module schedule already contains a
"Testing & Evaluation of AI Agent" lecture, so Lecture 10, Lab 5 and the
two-hour session are correctly positioned. All prior decks are in English, as
are our materials. The conflicts were in the *course shell*, not the content.

## Four classes of conflict and what was changed

| Class | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **P0 — factual** | Lecture 10 v6 slide 2 showed "Week 12 In class test" and "Week 9 Testing & EvaluationUI Design". The Week 9 deck had already announced the in-class test for Week 10 (2 hours, arrive 50 minutes early); Week 9 actually covers human-centred design. | Slide 2 replaced by "Where we are in the module": Week 9 = human-centred design, Week 10 = this lecture plus the test *per module schedule*, Week 12 = consolidation. **No date is written anywhere.** |
| Assessment | The official assessment is in-class test 35 (MCQ 20 + reflective 15) + group demo 65. There is no lab mark. Lab 5 still had four `[confirm: ...]` placeholders, a "draft for module leader confirmation" LO heading and three 100-mark routes. | Lab 5 v1.3: all placeholders resolved; **formative, no marks toward the module total**; the 100-mark rubrics are declared teaching feedback scales. |
| Numbering | Module LO A–E, Lab LO1–LO6 and Session LO1–LO6 shared the bare label "LO" with different meanings; Week 8's cover calls itself "Lecture 7". | Prefixes added everywhere (`Lab LO`, `Session LO`, `Module LO`), plus a module LO A–E mapping table in the Lab 5 brief. The name **"Lecture 10" is deliberately retained**; it is not renamed "Week 10 lecture". |
| Vocabulary | Four different "five dimensions"; HCI evaluation (SUS / ISO 9241-11) vs system-test evaluation; arXiv:2503.16416 (Yehudai et al., cited in Week 6) confused with arXiv:2507.21504 (cited in Lecture 10). | A **visible** Prior ISA104 Vocabulary card now closes both Lecture 10 v7 and the 91-slide session, and is rendered on the app's ISA104 Course page. The two surveys are explicitly disambiguated. |

## Content that is shared, not duplicated

- Week 5 pp. 33-38: RAG evaluation with four dimensions including
  counterfactual robustness — the direct ancestor of Lab 5's stratified dataset.
- Week 6 p. 33: "do not judge only end-to-end success, evaluate components" —
  the ancestor of the four-layer attribution in the two-hour session.
- Week 4 p. 40: red teaming / OWASP; Week 6 pp. 12-13: MCP; Week 9 pp. 18-19:
  confounders. These are referenced, not re-taught.
- Weeks 1-9 consistently frame work around **the student's own group agent**
  (group demo is 65% of the module), whereas Lab 5 uses fixed fixtures. Lab 5
  v1.3 therefore adds an optional, ungraded **Appendix C: transfer the method
  to your own group agent**.
